Research

Tissue images, spatial biology, and cancer biomarkers

My research connects routine H&E histology with molecular and spatial measurements to investigate the tumor microenvironment, prognosis, and treatment response across breast, ovarian, and head and neck cancers.

I work with whole-slide image analysis, weakly supervised deep learning, virtual staining, and generative AI. My earlier work in multimodal medical image fusion provides a foundation in computer vision, image segmentation, wavelets, and biomedical signal processing.

Current projects

AI-based virtual staining and immune profiling from H&E images

Developing H&E-based methods to infer macrophage and lymphocyte patterns and characterize the tumor microenvironment. Contributions include VISTA virtual staining (second author) and first-author VAIP and VIPER conference abstracts.

Tools: whole-slide imaging, computational pathology, virtual staining, deep learning, generative AI.

VISTA · VAIP · VIPER

Spatial proteomics, transcriptomics, and multi-omics

Integrating histology with spatial transcriptomics and spatial proteomics, alongside proteomic, genomic, RNA/transcriptomic, and DNA methylation/epigenomic data, to connect tissue architecture with molecular programs.

Methods: Visium, Visium HD, Xenium-relevant analyses, image-to-omics integration, and biomarker discovery.

Multi-cancer prognostic and predictive AI modeling

Investigating tumor architecture, stromal organization, collagen patterns, and immune-cell spatial relationships for cancer risk stratification and treatment-response research. Methods include model validation and survival analysis.

Tools: digital pathology, pathomics, multimodal AI, risk modeling, precision oncology.

Immune aging and cell senescence

Research interests in how immune states, senescence programs, and tissue microenvironment changes relate to cancer progression and treatment response.

Connecting tissue morphology, spatial biology, and molecular context.

Clinical translation

HistoRisk AI

Emory Innovation Challenge FY2026 · Selected and funded

I am a core computational pathology team member on HistoRisk AI, contributing to histopathologic breast cancer risk stratification from routine H&E slides.

My work spans collagen, nuclear, tumor-texture, and immune features; model development; validation; and interpretation. The project explores how image-derived biomarkers can complement molecular risk testing and support more accessible precision oncology.

Working across engineering, pathology, and oncology, I contribute to the development of scalable clinical decision-support approaches grounded in clinical needs.
